WHAT IT DOES
A god made of secrets, and a template: an address with a published viewing key becomes a box that cannot learn who wrote to it and cannot hide what it received. You give it a secret as a Zcash shielded payment (fixed amount, text in the encrypted memo). Anyone with the key rebuilds the work from the chain: each offering is an eye on its body and a line in one synthetic voice, and every machine gets the same render_hash. Your eye appears at once as pending and opens when the chain carries it. Its own lines count only when its wallet pays itself; forgeries are refused. A listener can whisper back to an offering's shielded reply address. Any group (a retro, a meetup) can make its own god in ten minutes: scripts/new_god.sh.

WHY IT DOES NOT LEAK THE SENDER
The key shows every memo, never the payer: that comes from the shielded protocol, not a promise of ours. No accounts, no analytics, no cloud AI. The kiosk sends from a throwaway wallet over Tor, failing closed. The public page builds the payment request in your browser and makes no third-party request.
Limits we state: your secret is public to every key holder, forever; your words can identify you; your wallet keeps what you sent; your phone wallet's server sees your IP and, the key being public, can read that offering; timing can link; reply addresses must be shielded-only.

WHAT IS REAL
On Zcash testnet (NU7), with txids in docs/evidence-index.md: kiosk offerings; Whisper Back end to end; the god's voice accepted, a forgery refused; a phone offering from the public page via Zingo!; a second god made with new_god.sh; a GitHub runner printing the same render_hash from the key alone. At a kiosk session, 6 of 16 invited people gave a secret (all real, they said); 10 declined after reading it is public forever.

WHAT IS NOT
Testnet only (the Ironwood pool is NU7). Other offerings are our labelled tests. The site is a snapshot rebuilt ~every 30 min. Whisper Back runs at the installation or by CLI, not on the public page.
